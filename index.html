Government & Opposition

Each row is a portfolio. Read across to see who holds it in every party. The party of the current Mayor is the Government, the runner-up at the last election is the Opposition, and any other party is on the Crossbench. Parties show as Undecided until an election has been run.

Parliament

Each party wins 1 seat per 10 votes (maximum 8). Its top vote-getters take the Senate seats (up to 4) automatically, and the Mayor always holds one of them. Any remaining seats are House seats, and the party chooses which of its other members fill them. Everyone else is Unseated and stays in the party until released to the Unassigned pool.

Bill tracker

Every bill and law in one place. Use a filter or the search box, and tap a row to jump to that bill. Amendment, repeal and reintroduction bills are linked to the bill they relate to.

Pass the bill

A bill starts in the House of Representatives. If the House passes it, it goes to the Senate, which can pass it, reject it, or pass it with an amendment that sends it back to the House. A bill that passes both houses goes to the Mayor, who assents (it becomes law) or vetoes it. Only members holding a seat can vote, and the Mayor has the casting vote in a tie. A defeated bill can be amended and returned to the House, and while the House is considering a revised bill or a Senate amendment it can request an amendment that sends the bill back to the Senate. Up to 3 amendment attempts are allowed, after which the bill stays Defeated. Amendments are drafted as a list of up to 3 at a time, which can be reviewed, edited and removed while the bill is in the House. A law is never edited directly: to change or remove one you introduce an amendment bill or a repeal bill, which goes through the same stages. A bill that is finally defeated or vetoed has to come back as a new bill.
A bill on a ministry's subject can only be introduced by the member whose role that is, for example the Police Minister for a bill on police checks. For anything that does not belong to one ministry, choose General, which any seated member can introduce. The sponsor must hold a seat in the Senate or the House. Members who hold a role but are Unseated are greyed out until they are seated.
